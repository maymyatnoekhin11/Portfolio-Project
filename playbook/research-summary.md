# 1. Purpose & Scope

This playbook provides a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for building an organic LinkedIn content strategy for B2B SaaS companies.

It is built from direct analysis of 10 LinkedIn practitioners, their published content, frameworks, and approaches. Additional insights were collected from YouTube transcripts and industry reports.

Every strategic recommendation in this document is connected to a source. Where experts provide different recommendations, this playbook evaluates the differences and explains the strategic decision taken.

This playbook is designed for B2B SaaS founders and marketers who want LinkedIn to generate authority, trust, relationships, and qualified business opportunities — not simply increase follower numbers.

The recommended execution commitment is approximately 45–60 minutes per day, assuming the company has genuine expertise, customer knowledge, or experience to share.

---

# 2. Objectives

The LinkedIn organic content strategy aims to:

1. Build credibility and authority with the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP).
2. Educate potential buyers through useful and relevant content.
3. Develop trust before sales conversations begin.
4. Create a sustainable content workflow that does not depend on viral performance.
5. Generate qualified inbound interest over a long-term period.

# 3. Phase 1 — Foundation: Audience & Profile

## 3.1 Define Your ICP Before Creating Content

One of the biggest mistakes in LinkedIn content strategy is creating content before understanding the audience.

Before publishing, define:

- Industry and company size of target customers.
- Job roles and decision-makers involved.
- Main business challenges.
- Customer motivations and desired outcomes.

Content should not focus only on product features. It should address the problems, motivations, and concerns of customers.

Ross Simmonds highlights that effective marketing requires understanding customer emotions and motivations rather than simply communicating product capabilities. Content becomes more effective when it connects with what customers genuinely care about.

**Source:** Ross Simmonds, LinkedIn post on emotional targeting (Talia Wolf)  
Link: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_emotional-targeting-talia-wolf-on-getting-activity-7490216306160459776-JyBV  
Collected: August 2026

## 3.2 Profile Optimization

A LinkedIn profile should function as a credibility page, not only a professional resume.

Before engaging with content, audiences usually evaluate whether the person or company appears trustworthy.

## Profile Checklist:

- Professional profile photo.
- Clear headline.
- Customer-focused About section.
- Evidence of expertise.
- Relevant achievements or examples.

The headline should clearly communicate:

**Who you help → What problem you solve → Why people should trust you**

Anthony Pierri emphasizes that SaaS messaging should be simple and customer-focused. Clear communication helps prospects quickly understand value and reduces confusion.

**Source:** Anthony Pierri, LinkedIn content analysis  
Link: https://www.linkedin.com/in/anthonypierri/recent-activity/all/  
Collected: June 2026

---

## 3.3 Develop a Clear Market Point of View

B2B SaaS companies should not only explain what their product does. They should also communicate their perspective on where the industry is moving.

April Dunford explains that during periods of major market change, companies need a clear point of view about the future direction of the market. A strong market perspective helps companies differentiate themselves and build customer confidence.

For SaaS companies, this means creating content around:

- Industry changes.
- Customer challenges.
- Future trends.
- Strategic opinions.

**Source:** April Dunford, LinkedIn post on market positioning during AI adoption  
Link: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aprildunford_when-we-have-big-changes-in-a-market-like-activity-7470826617833967616-mXuF

# 4. Phase 2 — Content Engine

## 4.1 Content Pillars

A B2B SaaS company should focus on three to four core content pillars.

| Pillar | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Educational Content | Build authority | Frameworks, tutorials, industry insights |
| Customer Problem Content | Show customer understanding | Challenges, mistakes, solutions |
| Personal Experience Content | Build trust | Lessons learned, failures, behind-the-scenes |
| Market Perspective Content | Develop thought leadership | Predictions, opinions, analysis |

These pillars combine insights from:

- Ross Simmonds: customer motivation and emotional relevance.
- Anthony Pierri: clear customer-focused messaging.
- April Dunford: market positioning.
- Tas Bober: consistent value-driven publishing.

---

# 4.2 Content Creation Framework

Every LinkedIn post should follow:

## Hook → Insight → Evidence → Discussion

## Hook

The opening sentence should create curiosity or relevance.

Examples:

- Counterintuitive statement.
- Specific result.
- Strong opinion.

## Insight

Clearly explain the main idea.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

## Evidence

Support the idea with:

- Data.
- Examples.
- Personal experience.
- Customer observations.

Justin Welsh highlights that AI has made content creation easier, but authentic expertise and original thinking remain the key differentiators.

AI should support content creation, not replace human experience.

**Source:** Justin Welsh, LinkedIn post on AI and authentic expertise  
Link: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/justinwelsh_ai-has-made-effort-really-cheap-thats-activity-7489311486817832961-ZKnh  
Collected: August 2026

## Discussion

End with:

- A question.
- An opinion request.
- A conversation starter.

---

# 4.3 Posting Cadence

Recommended starting schedule:

- Three posts per week.
- Increase to four or five posts when consistency is established.

Example:

**Monday:** Educational insight  
**Wednesday:** Industry perspective  
**Friday:** Personal lesson or case study  

Tas Bober's analysis of publishing 412 LinkedIn posts shows that consistent, valuable publishing is more effective than chasing viral performance.

Long-term credibility is built through consistency.

# 6. Where Experts Disagree

## Disagreement 1: AI-Assisted Content vs Human Expertise

### Justin Welsh

AI reduces the cost of creating content, making authentic experience and original thinking more valuable.

### Gaetano DiNardi

AI should be treated as a complementary channel while companies continue investing in proven organic strategies.

### My Decision

Both perspectives are valuable.

The recommended approach is to use AI as a productivity tool while ensuring every post contains human expertise, unique observations, and original thinking.

---

## Disagreement 2: Product Messaging vs Market Positioning

### Anthony Pierri

Companies need simple, clear messaging so customers quickly understand product value.

### April Dunford

Companies need a strong market perspective to differentiate themselves.

### My Decision

Both are necessary.

Clear messaging should come first because customers must understand the product. After that foundation is established, companies should build stronger market positioning.

---

## Disagreement 3: Content-Led Trust vs Outreach-Led Sales

### Mandy McEwen

LinkedIn content should build trust before sales conversations begin.

### Jason Bay

Outbound prospecting and proactive outreach remain important sales approaches.

### My Decision

For LinkedIn specifically, content should come first.

A visible and valuable content presence increases trust and improves the effectiveness of outreach.

---

# 7. What I Rejected and Why

## 1. Viral Content as the Main Strategy

Rejected because:

- Viral reach does not guarantee qualified leads.
- Large audiences may not represent target customers.
- B2B SaaS requires trust and expertise.

A smaller audience of decision-makers is more valuable than large irrelevant reach.

---

## 2. AI-Generated Content as a Volume Strategy

Rejected because:

- It can reduce authenticity.
- It does not demonstrate unique expertise.
- Buyers increasingly value original perspectives.

AI should assist creation, not replace human thinking.

# 8. My Original Idea

# Audience Journey Content Matrix

This playbook introduces a framework that connects LinkedIn content with buyer journey stages.

| Stage | Goal | Content Type |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Identify problems | Educational content |
| Consideration | Evaluate solutions | Frameworks and comparisons |
| Decision | Build confidence | Case studies and proof |
| Retention | Maintain relationships | Advanced insights |

## Why It Could Work

B2B buyers require different information at different stages.

Matching content with customer journey stages improves relevance and increases the chance of converting attention into business opportunities.

---

# 9. Weaknesses of This Playbook

This playbook has limitations:

1. The research is based on only 10 LinkedIn practitioners.
2. Most sources are English-language content and may not represent every market.
3. Expert opinions are based on selected posts, not complete career histories.
4. LinkedIn algorithms change frequently.
5. Organic content requires long-term consistency and may not create immediate pipeline.
6. Paid LinkedIn strategies are not included.
